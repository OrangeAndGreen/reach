Reach how high, how far

How far to the high ground?

For each distance from a place, the highest point anywhere within it. Cape Town has a mountain on its doorstep. Miami has to cross the sea to Cuba.

    Or click the map while this tab is open. Up to eight places.

      How far in a day's drive?

      Drive-time contours from one start, from road times to a grid of points around it. Then the highest ground inside each one.

        Or click the map while this tab is open.

        Road times come from the public OSRM demo server, so a run goes in polite batches. Times are free-flow driving with no traffic or stops.

        Print a piece of the planet

        A piece of real terrain as a closed STL, ready for the slicer. Pick the middle on the map, choose its shape and size, turn it to line up with the ground, and stretch the heights if the land is flat.

          Or click the map while this tab is open. Drag the dot to move it, or the outline to resize it.

          The sea floor comes from coarser tiles than the land (75 to 150 m per pixel, by latitude), so it prints softer. Lakes print as their flat surface.

          Rounded corners are slower to lift off the bed as the print cools. The ground within a corner is squeezed to fit, by up to 0.4 of the radius.

            A positive level in mm is a ridge that high, a negative one a groove that deep. The line runs along the land's edge, one grid point wide, so a finer detail draws a finer line. It follows the coast, not lakeshores, which the tiles hold as flat land.

            A cone cut up into the underside, its walls 30° from upright, so it prints without support and a pencil point reaches its tip. It goes up past the centre of mass where the model is thick enough, so the print hangs from the point and settles level; where it can't, it balances only with a steady hand. The walls and skins weigh more than the infill, so the centre of mass moves with the infill: set the slicer's. Any label moves aside to clear it.

            Holes cut down into the top to stand things in, such as SD cards or batteries, so each stands part way out, easy to grab. Each has a flat floor, its depth measured down from the lowest, average or highest ground under it.
